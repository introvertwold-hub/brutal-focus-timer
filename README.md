## 🧨 Why Another Timer?

Because every other timer on the market is a polite suggestion.  
They vibrate gently, they say “Great job!” when you finish a 20-minute nap disguised as a “focus session,” and they never—*never*—judge you when you close them after 42 seconds.

PUNCHCLOCK is the opposite. It’s built for people who have tried every soft, pastel-colored habit tracker and still end up doomscrolling at 2 AM. It’s built for the ones who need a verbal kick in the ass, not a gentle nudge. If you’ve ever said “I work better under pressure,” this is your pressure. On demand. Every single session.

This is not a tool. It’s a sparring partner. And it doesn’t pull punches.

## 🧊 The Problem With "Productivity Apps"

They’re designed by people who think a gold star is a sufficient reward system.  
They gamify everything *except* the actual work. They track your streaks, but they don’t tell you that your streak is built on 15-minute days. They give you a beautiful dashboard that shows you’re consistent—consistent at being mediocre.

PUNCHCLOCK doesn’t care about your streak. It cares about your **output**. Every timer session is a contract. When you break it, the app doesn’t just log a failure—it makes failure *uncomfortable*. Loud. Embarrassing. The kind of uncomfortable that rewires your brain so you stop clicking "start" unless you mean it.

## 💥 What PUNCHCLOCK Actually Does

It’s a simple premise, executed with brutal precision:

1. You set a timer for a work block.
2. The countdown begins.  
3. If you pause, cancel, or switch to a distracting app (configurable), the **shame sequence** triggers.
4. The shame sequence is a escalating cascade of:
   - A full-screen wallpaper that rotates through your *worst* life decisions (customizable, but the default set is harsh).
   - An optional voice line that asks, "Did you *really* need to check Twitter?"
   - A count-up timer showing how many seconds of your life you just wasted.
5. If you survive the shame and resume, the timer restarts **from zero**. Not from where you left off. From zero.
6. If you complete the session, you get a clean checkmark. No confetti. No fireworks. The reward is the work itself.

There is no "snooze." There is no "remind me later." There is only the clock, and your will to beat it.

## 🔥 The Pain Protocol

> *"The only thing harder than discipline is the regret of not having it."* — Someone who definitely missed a deadline.

PUNCHCLOCK introduces the **Pain Protocol**—the calibration system that determines *how* aggressive your sessions are.

- **Level 1 (The Cadet):** Mild. Timer beeps loudly. No pausing allowed. A nudge, not a shove.
- **Level 2 (The Sergeant):** Shame sequence activates on break. Custom wallpapers appear. Voice lines go from "You're better than this" to "Really? Again?"
- **Level 3 (The Drill Instructor):** Distraction detection is ON. Known sites are blocked at the network level (via hosts file). Closing the app triggers the anti-bribery ghost.
- **Level 4 (The General):** All previous, plus the timer **extends itself** if you abandon a session mid-way. You don’t just lose progress—you *gain* duty.
- **Level 5 (The Tyrant):** Only for the truly broken. Requires a physical USB button that must be pressed to confirm the session start. No button? No timer. Go do something else.

The Pain Protocol isn’t about being cruel. It’s about giving you the level of external pressure you *know* you need. Be honest about your discipline level, and choose accordingly.

## 📱 Responsive UI That Matches Your Mood

The interface reflects the energy of your session.

- **Focus Mode:** Minimal. White on black. Just the timer, the task, and the button.
- **Destruction Mode:** When a break is detected, the UI glitches. Colors invert. The timer shakes. It’s not pretty—because you’re not supposed to be pretty right now.
- **One-Handed Operation:** Designed for mobile and desktop. The start/stop button is the size of your thumb. The pause button is deliberately small and tucked away—encouraging you *not* to use it.
- **Battery-Friendly Dark UI:** OLED-optimized for maximum contrast and minimum battery drain. The only thing that should be draining is your will to procrastinate.

The UI isn’t here to impress you. It’s here to *remove friction* between intention and action. Fewer clicks, faster start, less time to second-guess yourself.

## 🛠 24/7 Accountability Engine

The timer is just the front door. Behind it is the **Accountability Engine**—a suite of features that keeps you honest even when the app is closed.

- **Session History Graph:** See your focus patterns over weeks. Notice the empty gaps where you *said* you’d work and didn’t.
- **Panic Mode Scheduler:** If you have a deadline in 6 hours, PUNCHCLOCK will automatically suggest "Panic Blocks" of 45 minutes with 5-minute breaks—and demand you start *now*, not in half an hour.
- **The Peer Edge (Local Only):** When you have the app open, it broadcasts "I'M FOCUSING" to local network devices. No internet needed. It’s a subtle flex for people at home who walk past your desk.
- **Failed Session Recaps:** Every session you abandon is logged with a timestamp and a note field. You fill in *why* you abandoned it. Over time, you see a pattern of your own bullshit excuses.

The Engine doesn’t work while you sleep. But it *remembers* what you did before you slept. And it will ask about it the next morning.

## 🚀 Usage — How to Stop Fucking Around

Here’s the entire flow in five bullet points. Read them. Follow them. Don’t reinvent the wheel.

1. **Open PUNCHCLOCK.**  
2. **Type your ONE task** in the box. If you can’t describe it in under 10 words, it’s too vague. Go break it down.
3. **Choose your duration.** Suggested default: 25 minutes. Don’t be a hero on day one.
4. **Hit the [START] button.** The timer starts. The shame sequence is armed.
5. **Do the task.** Nothing else. No checking email. No "quick questions." No phone calls.

When the timer ends, it announces completion. Then you get a 5-minute break. Then it asks if you want to "go again." It doesn’t *suggest*—it *asks*. And it stays in the foreground until you answer.

## 🎛 Configuration: Tune the Violence

Under the hood, everything is adjustable. The goal isn’t to fight the tool—it’s to tune it to your specific level of self-inflicted pain.

- **Pain Level:** 1 (Cadet) to 5 (Tyrant).
- **Shame Wallpaper Folder:** Drop your own "motivational" images. If you put a picture of your mortgage there, that’s your call.
- **Voice Lines:** Edit the text file that contains all the audio cues. Make them funnier, crueler, or completely silent.
- **Distraction List:** Manually add websites to the block list. The default list is conservative, but you know your weaknesses.
- **Auto-Extend Factor:** How many minutes to add to the *next* session when you fail.
- **Break Duration:** Default is 5 minutes. Go lower if you trust yourself. Go higher if you don’t.

There is no "cloud sync." Your data lives on your machine. If you want to share your failure stats with others, screenshot them—but you probably won’t.

## ⚠️ The "No Excuses" Disclaimer

**Read this before you complain.**

- PUNCHCLOCK is a **motivational tool**, not a medical one. If you have ADHD, anxiety, or other conditions that affect focus, this is *not* a replacement for professional advice. Use it as an *additional* layer, not a diagnosis.
- The shame sequence is **harsh by default**. It’s designed to be uncomfortable. If it makes you genuinely distressed, dial it down to Level 1 or 2. There’s no trophy for suffering if you quit entirely.
- The timer **will not magically make you work**. It’s a catalyst, not a substitute for action. If you click "start" and then stare at the wall for 25 minutes, that’s on you.
- **Privacy:** The app works offline. It does *not* send your task lists or focus logs anywhere. The only "network" feature is optional local broadcast, which you can disable in settings.
- **No Liability:** The creators assume zero responsibility for missed deadlines, red-eye nights, or abandoned projects— *especially* if you reached Level 4 and still failed. At that point, we’d like to talk.

This tool is a hammer. It can build a house, or it can smash your standing desk. The choice is always yours.
